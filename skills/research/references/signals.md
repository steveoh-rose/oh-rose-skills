# Signals and reading results

## Signals by method

**Competitor and analogous research**
- Steps, clicks or screens to complete equivalent tasks
- Feature parity: present, absent, partial
- Heuristic violations across comparable flows

**Support tickets and feedback**
- Ticket volume by category as a share of contacts
- Repeat contacts for the same issue
- Customer Effort Score after resolution
- Time to resolution for UX problems

**Field study**
- Interruptions during critical tasks
- Switches between tools
- Re-entering or copying information between systems

**Preference test**: selection split, reasons
**5-second test**: purpose recall, action recall
**Tree test**: correct destination, success without backtracking, first-choice accuracy
**Card sort**: grouping agreement, cluster cohesion, confidence
**First-click test**: accuracy, time to first click, click distribution
**Unmoderated prototype test**: completion, dead clicks, drop-off, perceived difficulty
**Moderated prototype test**: completion with and without help, critical incidents, hesitation, perceived difficulty

## Proxies
When direct user access is limited (common in enterprise B2B), talk to sales, customer success and account managers. Use them to find recurring friction and deal-breakers. Keep proxy interpretation separate from direct user evidence and label it.

## Reading results
- Behaviour first, opinion second.
- State sample size and who took part.
- Say what the result cannot tell you.
- Tie it back to the decision it was meant to change.
- Do not use survey counts as a substitute for understanding why.
