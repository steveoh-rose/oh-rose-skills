# Design principles (full list)

Apply in order. Earlier principles win when two conflict.

1. **Solve the job to be done.** Remove elements that do not make the user's path clearer or shorter.
2. **Balance goals honestly.** Prioritise the user's goal. Business and personal preferences should not obscure it.
3. **One clear hierarchy.** One primary emphasis per section or screen.
4. **Make the path legible.** Users know where to start, where a link leads, and when an action is complete.
5. **Be honest.** Labels, status, claims and interactions represent what is happening.
6. **Respect the reading path.** Predictable alignment. No visual backtracking.
7. **Plain language.** Labels describe outcomes, not vague actions.
8. **Discoverable interaction.** Never rely on hover alone to reveal links or controls.
9. **Semantic colour.** Status colours communicate status, not decoration.
10. **Space before boxes and lines.** Group with spacing first. Dividers and filled cards only for meaningful boundaries.
11. **Stable layout.** Hover, loading, validation and success do not move surrounding content.
12. **Immediate feedback.** Acknowledge input promptly and honestly.
13. **Reversible actions.** Do not discard input without warning.
14. **No dead ends.** Every page or state offers a next step or a way back.
15. **Guard consequential actions.** Destructive or irreversible actions are unmistakable and deliberate.
16. **Animate with purpose.** Explain a relationship, acknowledge input, or support a wait.
17. **No shouting.** No all-caps styling. Use size, weight and hierarchy.
18. **Give important work room.** Primary content gets the largest reasonable space.
19. **Remove unnecessary steps.** Sensible defaults. Ask only for what is needed.
20. **No tutorial required.** Understandable without coach marks.
21. **Stay consistent.** Same visual and interaction language throughout.
22. **Adapt, do not redesign.** Responsive layouts keep meaning and identity.
23. **Spend attention deliberately.** Primary content visible. Secondary content behind clear, visible entry points only when needed.
24. **Preserve state across changes.** Resize or rotation does not lose focus, input or progress.
25. **Size to content.** No fixed empty space or oversized containers.

## Human-centred principles

For product, interaction and AI decisions. Name the user goal, the principle, and the evidence or constraint behind the choice.

1. **Solve the right problem.** (Mental models, Jakob's Law)
2. **Make the next step obvious.** (Recognition over recall, Hick's Law, Fitts's Law)
3. **Make the important things matter.** (Gestalt, Von Restorff effect, salience bias)
4. **Make behaviour predictable.** (Jakob's Law, law of similarity, human-AI interaction guidelines)
5. **Be honest about what is happening.** (Framing effect, transparency and explainability)
6. **Keep people in control.** (Status quo bias, user control and freedom)
7. **Prevent mistakes and make failure recoverable.** (Postel's Law, error recovery, NIST AI RMF)
8. **Earn trust through usefulness.** (Aesthetic-usability effect, NIST AI RMF)

For AI features, check 4, 5, 6 and 7 first: predictable, honest, controllable, recoverable.

Reference: lawsofux.com, Microsoft Human-AI Interaction Guidelines, NIST AI Risk Management Framework.
